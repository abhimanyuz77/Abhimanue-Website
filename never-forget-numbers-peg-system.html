Never Forget Numbers Again! Discover the Peg System

By Abhimanue Udayan November 15, 2026
Free Video Lesson · 5 min

Ever struggled to remember numbers, dates, sequences or important facts? Repeating them over and over isn't always the best approach. What if you could turn numbers into memorable mental pictures?

That's where the Peg System comes in!

The Peg System is a memory technique that connects numbers with familiar objects, allowing you to attach new information to something your brain can easily visualise.

How Does It Work?

We associate numbers with rhyming words that act as memory hooks:

  • 1Bun
  • 2Shoe
  • 3Tree
  • 4Door
  • 5Hive
  • 6Sticks
  • 7Heaven
  • 8Gate
  • 9Wine
  • 10Hen

Once you learn these pegs, you can create unusual, exaggerated mental images to connect them with the information you want to remember.

For example, to remember that oxygen has the atomic number 8, you might picture a giant gate made entirely of oxygen cylinders swinging open in front of you.

The stranger and more exaggerated the image, the better it sticks.

Where Can You Use It?

  • Remembering atomic numbers in Chemistry
  • Learning important historical dates and years
  • Memorising mathematical values and constants
  • Remembering steps in biological processes
  • Recalling important points in examination answers and presentations

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Peg System?

The Peg System is a memory technique where each number is linked to a fixed, easy-to-picture word — usually one that rhymes with it, like 1–Bun, 2–Shoe, 3–Tree. These words act as "pegs" or hooks. To remember new information, you create a vivid mental image that connects the peg word with what you want to recall.

How do I remember numbers larger than 10?

You can extend the system with your own pegs for 11, 12, 13 and beyond, or combine pegs to build longer numbers. For a 4-digit year like 1947, picture a story that links the pegs for 19 and 47 in sequence. The key is always the same: turn each number into an object, then link the objects with an exaggerated image or story.

Why do exaggerated images work better than repetition?

Your brain is far better at storing vivid, unusual pictures than abstract digits. Repeating a number relies on rote rehearsal, which fades quickly. A strange, funny or dramatic image creates a strong association that your memory can grab onto — which is why the Peg System helps information stick with far less effort.
