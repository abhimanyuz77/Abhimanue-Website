How to Handle Mental Chatter When Studying

By Abhimanue Sir October 7, 2026
Free Video Lesson · 7 min

Have you ever sat down to study, only to find your mind immediately coming up with reasons to stop?

"I'm tired."
"Maybe I should check Instagram."
"I'll start in ten minutes."
"This chapter is boring."
"What if I don't understand this?"

Before you know it, several minutes have disappeared.

The problem isn't that your mind talks. Your mind is supposed to talk. The problem begins when you start treating every thought as an instruction.

In this lesson, you'll learn how to create some distance between you and your thoughts, recognize mental chatter without automatically obeying it, and bring your attention back to what you actually decided to do.

You'll Learn How To:

  • Recognize when your mind is getting distracted
  • Separate thoughts from instructions
  • Stop negotiating with yourself
  • Handle urges to check your phone or seek stimulation
  • Deal with thoughts like "I'm not smart enough" or "I'm going to fail"
  • Use "That's a thought, not a fact" when negative thoughts appear
  • Focus on the next question, page, problem, or ten minutes
  • Return to your task without waiting for motivation
  • Train your attention by repeatedly bringing it back

The Goal Isn't a Silent Mind

The goal isn't to make your mind completely silent. The goal is simple:

When your mind wanders, notice it and return faster.

Your mind can be an incredibly powerful tool. It can help you learn, create, solve problems, remember information, imagine possibilities, and plan your future.

But your mind is a tool, not your master.

The next time your mind says, "I don't feel like doing this," you don't need to fight it. You can simply say:

"I heard you. But not now."

Then return to what you decided to do.

Learn how to learn. Learn how to direct your attention.
